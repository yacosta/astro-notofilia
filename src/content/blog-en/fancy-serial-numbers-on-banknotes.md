Two identical banknotes can be worth very different amounts for a single
detail: their **serial number**. Certain combinations are so sought after that
they multiply the price of a piece, often far above face value. These are the
most prized types.

## Low serials

The lowest numbers —**00000001, 00000002, 00000003**…— are the first printed
of an issue and, for their rarity and symbolism, are highly valued. The lower
the number, the greater the interest.

## Solids

All digits the same: **7777777**, **5555555**. They are extremely scarce and
eye-catching, hence the strong demand.

## Radars (palindromes)

The number reads the same left to right as backwards, such as **1234321** or
**4567654**. They are called *radar* or *palindrome* notes and are a classic of
serial collecting.

## Ladders

Digits in ascending or descending sequence: **1234567** or **7654321**. They
are very visual and hard to find without wear.

## Repeaters and other curiosities

Patterns such as **123123123**, repeated pairs, or recognizable dates also
attract interest. In some countries there are also **replacement notes** (with
a star or special mark in the serial), printed to replace defective pieces and
therefore scarcer.

## Condition still rules

An extraordinary serial number lifts the value, but conservation remains
decisive: a spectacular serial on a heavily circulated note is worth far less
than on an uncirculated one. The combination of **fancy serial + excellent
grade** is what reaches the highest prices, as with
[the $2 note with serial L00000002A](/noticias/billete-2-dolares-serie-baja/)
we covered in News.
