Starting a banknote collection is simpler —and cheaper— than many people think.
Notaphily can begin with what you already carry in your wallet and grow at your
own pace. These are the keys to taking the first steps well.

## 1. Choose a focus

Collecting “every banknote in the world” is overwhelming. Collectors enjoy it
more when they pick a thread: a **country** (Colombia, for example), a
**historical period**, a **theme** (portraits, fauna, trains), an **issuing
bank**, or a type such as **polymer** notes. A clear focus gives the collection
coherence and makes buying decisions easier.

## 2. Where to find pieces

The first pieces often come from **daily change**, **family albums**, or notes
that have left circulation. Later, many collectors turn to **numismatic fairs**,
**auction houses**, and **specialist dealers**. Always buy from trusted sources
and, for expensive pieces, prefer those with clear backing or provenance.

## 3. Condition is everything

In notaphily, two identical notes can be worth very different amounts according
to grade. They are classified on a scale that runs, broadly, from *Uncirculated*
(UNC) to *Good*, passing through *About Uncirculated*, *Extremely Fine*, and
*Fine*. Folds, stains, tears, and worn corners reduce value. At the start, learn
to see these details before you buy.

## 4. Handling and storage

Paper money is fragile. Handle notes with clean, dry hands —or with gloves—
hold them by the edges, and store them in **acid-free sleeves** (no PVC). Avoid
direct sunlight, humidity, and heat. Never glue them, mark them, or “restore”
them with tape: that destroys their value.

## 5. Classify and document

Keep a record of each piece: country, issuer, denomination, year, catalog number
(banknotes are referenced by **Pick** number) and grade. A good catalog and an
ordered inventory turn a pile of notes into a real collection.
